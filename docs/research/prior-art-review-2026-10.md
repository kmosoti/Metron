# Cognitive Kernel: A Source-Verified Prior-Art Review for Multi-Representation, Resource-Bounded Learning

The core critique holds: as specified, the hidden-Boolean-function laboratory cannot test H1, H2 or H4. At n≤4 the 16-query budget already covers the whole truth table. Greedy disagreement-based querying is a provably near-optimal baseline for exact identification of a finite class. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Analysis-of-a-greedy-active-learning-strategy-Dasgupta/8a2305b3867547c1da624b06b5a45143cf531c4c) And every view is exact and cheap at this scale, so there is no cost asymmetry for routing to exploit. The architecture's individual mechanisms all have strong prior art: representation selection (rep2rep), rational metareasoning (Lieder, Griffiths and Callaway), algorithm selection (ASlib), impasse-driven chunking (Soar), utility learning plus production compilation (ACT-R), oracle-guided synthesis (Jha et al.) and library learning (DreamCoder and Stitch). What is defensibly new is the evaluation discipline: matched-budget ablations, provenance-aware evidence, and transform contracts. It is not a new cognitive mechanism.

## TL;DR

- **The lab must change before H1, H2 and H4 mean anything.** At n=4, 16 queries is the full truth table, and all 65,536 functions fit in about 128 KB. Hegedűs (COLT 1995) brackets the optimal membership-query count between max{XTD, log₂|C|} and XTD·log₂|C|, and greedy splitting is near-optimal in the noise-free setting (Dasgupta, NIPS 2004; Golovin & Krause, COLT 2010). So representation choice can only change *compute* or *inductive bias*, not information. Use n=5–8, mixed structural families (parity/affine, monotone, read-once, threshold, k-term DNF, decision trees) and binding enumeration budgets.
- **Closest prior art, by mechanism:** rep2rep / Representational Systems Theory (Jamnik, Cheng, Raggi, Stockdill) for representation selection. Lieder & Griffiths (2017, 2020) and Callaway et al. (UAI 2018) for resource-bounded computation selection. Soar and ACT-R for learned routing plus composition. Jha et al. (ICSE 2010) for exactly the Section 11 "distinguishing input" loop. DreamCoder and Stitch for capability construction. The combination is unusual, but no single component is novel.
- **Measure headroom before building learners.** Report the ASlib single-best vs. virtual-best "gap closed"; the best systems in the 2017 Algorithm Selection Competition still left a 38% gap (Lindauer, van Rijn & Kotthoff, arXiv 1805.01214). Use an oracle-optimal query tree or the Hegedűs bracket as the query-count ruler. Use a contextual-bandit and Lieder-style Bayesian strategy-selection model as the control for the three-factor rule. Use NPN canonicalization for split hygiene. Report results rliable-style (IQM, stratified bootstrap). Compare difficulty orderings against public human data: Piantadosi et al. 2016 learning curves, and the Nosofsky et al. 1994 aggregates in R's catlearn.

## Key Findings

### 1. What is settled (and therefore must be a baseline, not a hypothesis)

- **Exact identification with membership queries only is characterized for finite classes.** Hegedűs (COLT 1995, pp. 108–117) defines the *extended teaching dimension* (XTD). [purdue](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~hanneke/docs/2007/hanneke-teaching-dimension.pdf) Informally, XTD is the worst case, over any function (even one outside the class), of the smallest set of inputs whose labels either pin down that function within the class or rule out every candidate. As restated by Hanneke (COLT 2007), Hegedűs proves max{XTD, log₂|C|} ≤ #MQ(C) ≤ XTD·log₂|C|. The upper bound is achieved by a halving-style algorithm that queries a minimal specifying set for the majority-vote hypothesis. [purdue](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~hanneke/docs/2007/hanneke-teaching-dimension.pdf) This is a *bracket*, not an exact optimum.
- **Greedy "split the survivors" querying is near-optimal in the noise-free case.** Dasgupta (NIPS 2004, "Analysis of a greedy active learning strategy") proves that the greedy splitting rule is approximately as good as any strategy for minimizing labels. [nips](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2636-analysis-of-a-greedy-active-learning-strategy) The guarantee is a multiplicative factor logarithmic in the hypothesis-class size, for average-case cost under a prior. Golovin & Krause (COLT 2010, "Adaptive Submodularity") recover and generalize this result. [learningtheory](https://www.learningtheory.org/colt2010/papers/111golovin.pdf) Nowak (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 57(12):7893–7906, 2011) shows that generalized binary search (GBS) reaches the information-theoretic O(log N) only under specific geometric and incoherence conditions, not in general. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.4397) Golovin, Krause & Ray (NIPS 2010) show that GBS can perform very poorly once observations are noisy. [acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/2997189.2997275)
- **One critique citation is mis-applied.** Hellerstein, Pillaipakkamnatt, Raghavan & Wilkins ("How many queries are needed to learn?", JACM 43(5):840–862, 1996) characterize query complexity via polynomial certificates. Their setting has membership *and equivalence* queries. [springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-0-387-30162-4_66) It is relevant background, but it does not bound the user's membership-only lab.
- **An unrestricted class leaves nothing to optimize.** If the class is all 2^(2^n) functions, then log₂|C| = 2^n, and every strategy must query every input. Any advantage in the lab comes entirely from the *bounded expression grammar*, i.e., from prior structure. This is the most important conceptual point for H1.

### 2. What the lab critique (A) gets right, and what it needs refining

- **Correct:** with 16 queries at n=4, the budget equals the truth table. With n≤4, a brute-force version space over all 65,536 functions (16 bits each) is about 128 KB and is evaluated exhaustively in microseconds. That makes it the "best single representation", and it is optimal in information terms. All views (syntax tree, circuit, truth table, constraints) are exact and interconvertible at negligible cost.
- **Refinement 1:** representations do not change the *information* a query yields. They change (a) the *compute* needed to maintain the version space and pick a split, and (b) the *inductive bias*, meaning which consistent hypothesis is preferred when predicting unqueried inputs before identification is complete. H1 must therefore be scored on compute-to-answer and on pre-identification prediction accuracy. Interventions alone will not show it.
- **Refinement 2:** cost asymmetry appears naturally from n=5. There are 2^32 ≈ 4.3×10⁹ functions, about 17 GB as explicit truth tables. At n=6 there are 2^64, so explicit version spaces become impossible and grammar-restricted, representation-specific reasoning becomes necessary.
- **Refinement 3:** representations have genuinely different costs for specific families. Darwiche & Marquis ("A knowledge compilation map", JAIR 17:229–264, 2002) tabulate which queries and transformations are polytime for each language (DNF, CNF, OBDD, d-DNNF and others), along with their relative succinctness. [jair](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10311) Two well-known examples follow. Parity needs 2^(n−1) DNF terms, but it is a single linear form in algebraic normal form (ANF, the GF(2) polynomial). Affine functions are identified with n+1 membership queries: query the zero vector and the n unit vectors. Mixed families are what make routing matter.

**What is plausibly new:** (i) explicit *transform contracts* that record lost information and effect class for each operator; (ii) a provenance rule that redescriptions of one observation are not independent evidence; (iii) a pre-registered protocol that ablates each mechanism independently, with matched budgets, in a single replayable kernel. These are contributions to *methodology and engineering*. Present them that way.

## Details

### Closest prior work to the combined design

- **rep2rep / Representational Systems Theory (Jamnik, Cheng, Raggi, Stockdill; Cambridge/Sussex).** This is the closest match to the thesis of "adaptive selection of useful representations". RST (arXiv 2206.03172, 2022 preprint) encodes representational systems as grammatical, entailment and identification spaces, built from "construction spaces". [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03249) It supports full and partial structural transformation between systems. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03172) "Correspondence-based analogies for choosing problem representations" (VL/HCC 2020, doi 10.1109/VL/HCC50065.2020.9127258) scores the informational suitability and cognitive cost of a representation and compares its recommendations against 11 experts. [advait](https://advait.org/files/stockdill_2020_correspondences2.pdf) [diagrams-conference](https://diagrams-conference.org/2021/2021/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Stockdill.pdf) Oruga (Diagrams 2024, doi 10.1007/978-3-031-66997-2_20; extended version arXiv 2509.04041) is the implementation, [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04041) [springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-66997-2_20) and "Structure Transfer" (arXiv 2509.03249, 2025) adds an inference calculus. *Overlap:* views, frames, transformations, selection by suitability and cost. *Difference:* rep2rep is aimed at human users and expert-validated recommendation. It does not learn routing from experience under a compute budget, and it does not run active experiments. Your Frames and transform contracts should be explicitly mapped onto RST constructs. Otherwise reviewers will see a reinvention.
- **Resource-rational metareasoning (Lieder, Griffiths, Callaway).** Lieder & Griffiths 2017 (doi 10.1037/rev0000075) model strategy selection as learning a predictive model of each strategy's reward and cost from problem features, then choosing by value of computation. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Strategy-Selection-as-Rational-Metareasoning-Lieder-Griffiths/784f1f6d9c28c7a4b780047846125a22a1ab0549) Lieder & Griffiths 2020 (BBS 43, doi 10.1017/S0140525X1900061X) give the general methodology of resource-rational analysis. [cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/resourcerational-analysis-understanding-human-cognition-as-the-optimal-use-of-limited-computational-resources/586866D9AD1D1EA7A1EECE217D392F4A) Callaway et al. 2018 (UAI, pp. 776–785, arXiv 1711.06892) formalize computation selection as a meta-level MDP. [github](https://github.com/microprediction/metareasoning/issues/3) They derive a sample-efficient learning algorithm (BMPS) whose value-of-computation estimate lies between the myopic value and the value of perfect information, and it beats the meta-greedy heuristic. [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/uai/2018/callaway2018uai-learning/) Milli, Lieder & Griffiths (AAAI 2017) analyze when bounded-optimal metareasoning favors few cognitive systems, [cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/resourcerational-analysis-understanding-human-cognition-as-the-optimal-use-of-limited-computational-resources/586866D9AD1D1EA7A1EECE217D392F4A) which is a direct warning for H2: when metareasoning overhead is non-trivial, a small fixed repertoire can be optimal. *Overlap:* nearly all of Section 6 and Section 9. *Difference:* their object-level problems are planning and decision tasks rather than hidden-function identification with multiple exact representations.
- **Soar and ACT-R.** In Soar, all subgoals arise automatically from impasses, [academia](https://www.academia.edu/124913711/SOAR_An_architecture_for_general_intelligence) and chunking converts subgoal results into new rules. Your stopping conditions (NoApplicableOperator, Stalled) are impasses, and your composition promotion is chunking with an added validation gate. ACT-R's subsymbolic utility learning picks which production fires, [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15482072/) and production compilation merges sequential productions. That is your routing (Section 9) plus capability construction (Section 10). *Difference:* your design separates the routing graph from the knowledge model and requires held-out validation before promotion. Soar chunking and ACT-R compilation are unvalidated and automatic. That is a real but modest design distinction.
- **Oracle-guided synthesis.** Jha et al. 2010 (doi 10.1145/1806799.1806833) synthesize loop-free bit-manipulating programs from components. At each step they use an SMT solver to find a "distinguishing input" on which two consistent programs differ, query the I/O oracle, and repeat. [sri](https://www.sri.com/publication/oracle-guided-component-based-program-synthesis/) [berkeley](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sseshia/pubdir/synth-icse10.pdf) Section 11's loop is this algorithm with enumeration in place of SMT. Jha & Seshia 2017 (Acta Informatica 54(7):693–726, doi 10.1007/s00236-017-0294-5) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07451) give the general theory of oracle-guided inductive synthesis (OGIS). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.07451) Polgreen, Reynolds & Seshia's "Synthesis modulo oracles" (2022) generalizes to black-box oracles. [acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-030-94583-1_13)
- **Program-induction models of concept learning.** Goodman et al. 2008 and Piantadosi et al. 2016 (Psych. Rev. 123(4):392–424, doi 10.1037/a0039980) define probabilistic grammars over Boolean formulas and fit them to human learning curves. Piantadosi et al. find that people's inferences match a *rich, non-minimal* set of Boolean primitives. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301300276_The_Logical_Primitives_of_Thought_Empirical_Foundations_for_Compositional_Cognitive_Models) [nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27077241/) This is directly relevant to choosing your grammar.
- **Other architectures (Sigma, Clarion, LIDA, OpenCog/Hyperon, Icarus, NARS, Spaun; Gödel machine/OOPS; AIXI approximations).** I did not verify these against primary sources in this pass. From general knowledge: Spaun (Eliasmith et al. 2012) uses VSA-style semantic pointers. LIDA implements global workspace theory. The Gödel machine permits self-modification of everything, including its utility function given a proof of benefit. Your rule that the evaluator, hidden cases and promotion rules are immutable is the deliberate opposite of the Gödel machine. Treat these as positioning references, not overlap claims, until checked.

### Hypothesis-by-hypothesis bounds, baselines, pitfalls

**H1 (representation selection beats the best fixed representation).**
(a) Bound: query count is bounded by Hegedűs's XTD bracket regardless of representation. Only compute and prior can differ. Knowledge-compilation succinctness separations (Darwiche & Marquis 2002) predict *where* compute gains exist: parity in DNF versus ANF, monotone functions as minimal-true/maximal-false point sets (Bioch & Ibaraki, Inf. & Comp. 123(1):50–63, 1995), [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0890540185711571) and OBDD variable-order sensitivity.
(b) Strongest baseline: the per-family virtual best representation (VBS) and the single best (SBS), both at matched compute.
(c) Pitfall: at n≤4 the truth-table version space dominates, so H1 is trivially false or trivially true depending on how compute is counted. Pre-register the cost model (operator calls, expansions, wall time).

**H2 (experience-conditioned routing beats a hand-authored schedule).**
(a) Bound: routing can at most close the SBS–VBS gap. In the 2017 Algorithm Selection Competition (Lindauer, van Rijn & Kotthoff, "The Algorithm Selection Competitions 2015 and 2017", arXiv 1805.01214), the best systems still left a 38% gap. Over the runtime scenarios, the VBS averaged a 31.8-fold speedup over the SBS, so headroom varies hugely by scenario.
(b) Baselines: a fixed schedule; an ε-greedy or tabular contextual policy; a linear contextual bandit (LinUCB, Li et al. WWW 2010; not re-verified here); the Lieder & Griffiths 2017 rational-metareasoning model.
(c) Pitfalls: (1) algorithm-selection overfitting on small instance sets. ASlib (Bischl et al., AIJ 237:41–58, 2016, doi 10.1016/j.artint.2016.04.003) exists precisely to standardize cross-validated evaluation, and it scores selectors as gap closed between SBS (0) and VBS (1). [ubc](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~kevinlb/papers/2016-AIJ-ASlib.pdf) [liacs](https://ada.liacs.nl/papers/BisEtAl16.pdf) (2) Credit assignment: your rule w_ij ← clip((1−λ)w_ij + η·e_ij·(r − b)) is REINFORCE-with-baseline (Williams 1992) with accumulating traces. With one terminal reward per ~16-step episode it reduces to Monte Carlo credit, and it smears credit over every transition used. (3) Milli et al. 2017 predict that a learned router with real overhead can lose to a small fixed repertoire.

**H3 (reusable compositions improve transfer or inference cost).**
(a) Prediction: compression-based library learning (DreamCoder; Stitch, which its authors report is 3–4 orders of magnitude faster and uses 2 orders of magnitude less memory than DreamCoder's deductive compressor at comparable or better compressivity, POPL 2023, doi 10.1145/3571234) helps only when tasks share recurring substructure.
(b) Baseline: the expanded (inlined) reference execution at equal compute, which your design already includes. Keep it as the primary comparator.
(c) Negative results: Berlot-Attwell, Rudzicz & Si ("Library Learning Doesn't", NeurIPS 2024 MATH-AI workshop, arXiv 2410.20274) found that in two reported-successful systems, LEGO-Prover and TroVE, function reuse was extremely infrequent on miniF2F and MATH, and that self-correction and self-consistency were the primary drivers of the reported gains. Their follow-up (arXiv 2504.03048, 2025) found that LEGO-Prover's accuracy gains vanished once compute was accounted for. [bytez](https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2504.03048/paper) Sesterhenn et al. (arXiv 2507.22069, 2025) ran a compute-matched re-evaluation of TroVE. [dblp](https://dblp.org/pid/281/1624.html) These systems are LLM-based, but the lesson transfers directly: measure *reuse frequency* on held-out tasks and match compute.

**H4 (HDC retrieval beats exact or vector retrieval per unit resource).**
(a) Theory: Clarkson, Ubaru & Yang ("Capacity Analysis of Vector Symbolic Architectures", arXiv 2301.10352; JAIR 2026) bound the dimension needed for set-membership and intersection-size queries in MAP-I, MAP-B and sparse binary VSAs. They connect VSAs to sketching and Bloom filters. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10352)
(b) Baselines: exact bitmask filtering, a Bloom filter, and an ordinary ANN index.
(c) Pitfall: at n=6 an exact truth table is 64 bits, while an 8,192-bit hypervector is 128× larger than the object it sketches. HDC cannot win on recall per byte for the lab's native objects. Crosstalk grows with the number of bundled items. Kleyko et al.'s Part II survey covers known limitations. [arxiv](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2112.15424v2) (Unreviewed capacity "laws" circulating in GitHub issues should not be used.)

### Cognitive-science framing and human data

- **Resource rationality** (Lieder & Griffiths 2020) is the right umbrella framing. Your system is a candidate *algorithmic-level* model of bounded inquiry. Griffiths et al. 2019 ("Doing more with less", Curr. Opin. Behav. Sci. 29:24–30) connect meta-reasoning and meta-learning across humans and machines. Callaway et al. (Nature Human Behaviour, 2022) show resource-rational planning in people. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23519) Recent work on planning-strategy discovery (arXiv 2412.03111; arXiv 2505.23519) models how people learn new strategies, which is the cognitive analog of H2 and H3.
- **Human inquiry.** Coenen, Nelson & Gureckis ("Asking the right questions about the psychology of human inquiry: Nine open challenges", Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 26(5):1548–1587, 2019; doi 10.3758/s13423-018-1470-5) [jst](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201902286686957781) review the optimal-experiment-design (OED) account of human question-asking. They argue that OED misses much of what is hard: where hypotheses come from and how questions are generated. [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-018-1470-5) This supports keeping hypothesis generation as an explicit, removable operator.
- **Boolean concept difficulty.** Feldman 2000 (Nature 407:630–633, doi 10.1038/35036586) reports that difficulty is proportional to Boolean complexity, the length of the shortest equivalent formula. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Minimization-of-Boolean-complexity-in-human-concept-Feldman/5b81efddb8f201ad2b147642eaa12aaac36e9231) *Caveat:* Vigo (2006, J. Math. Psych.) showed that Feldman's reported values were not minimal for 9 of 41 types, [ohio](https://research.ohio.edu/files/vigo-ronaldo/files/2006-vigo-notecomplexitybooleanconcepts-jmathpsych.pdf) and a later reanalysis revised the complexity of 148 of 253 concept types. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222676408_A_note_on_the_complexity_of_Boolean_concepts) Kurtz et al. 2013 (JEP:LMC 39(2):552–572, doi 10.1037/a0029178) found that the classic SHJ ordering is not invariant across procedures. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22154) [berkeley](https://colala.berkeley.edu/) Nosofsky et al. 1994: block-level mean error by SHJ type (I–VI, 16 blocks, N=40/type), shipped as data(nosof94) in the R package catlearn along with ALCOVE and SUSTAIN simulations. ## Recommendations

1. **Oracle-optimal query ruler.** For n≤4 with your grammar, compute the exact optimal adaptive query tree by dynamic programming over version spaces. Report each agent's interventions as a ratio to that optimum. For n≥5, report the Hegedűs bracket max{XTD, log₂|C|} … XTD·log₂|C| for the grammar-restricted class. XTD is computable for a finite class, but each specifying-set computation is a set-cover problem, so expect brute force to be feasible only for small grammars. Always include greedy splitting as a baseline. Since it is near-optimal, beating it on interventions is not a reasonable H1/H2 target. Beating it on compute is.
2. **Headroom first (before M2).** Run every fixed representation and route on a development family and compute SBS and VBS. If VBS−SBS is small, H2 is dead on arrival and you should not build plastic routing. Report learned routing as ASlib-style "gap closed".
3. **Redesign the lab for cost asymmetry.** Use n∈{5,6,7,8}. Mix families: parity/affine (cheap in ANF, exponential in DNF), monotone (cheap as minimal-true points), read-once, threshold, k-term DNF and small decision trees. Impose a binding enumeration/expansion budget so the truth-table version space is unaffordable. Score both interventions and compute-to-answer.
4. **Human external-validity check.** Fit the agent's pre-identification prediction accuracy curves to Piantadosi et al.'s 34 Boolean concepts and to the Nosofsky 1994 SHJ means. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22154) [rstudio](https://cran.rstudio.com/web/packages/catlearn/catlearn.pdf) Report rank correlation of difficulty. Given Vigo's and Kurtz's revisions, pre-register that a mismatch is informative rather than a failure.
5. **NPN deduplication.** Canonicalize every generated function under input permutation, input negation and output negation before splitting train/test/final families, so that no held-out function is an NPN-relative of a training function. (OEIS A000370 lists 222 NPN classes at n=4 and 616,126 at n=5.)
6. **A real H4 workload.** Cross-episode retrieval: given k observed (input, output) pairs at n=6–8, retrieve previously solved functions consistent with them from a store of 10⁴–10⁶ entries. Baselines are an exact bitmask scan, a Bloom filter, and an ANN index. Measure recall@k vs. bytes and nanoseconds. Drop H4 if the exact scan wins at your scale.
7. **Controls for the three-factor rule.** Compare against (i) a tabular/linear contextual policy without traces, (ii) LinUCB, and (iii) a Lieder & Griffiths 2017-style Bayesian strategy-performance regression. Use identical features and training episodes. Frame the Hebbian rule honestly as REINFORCE-with-baseline plus eligibility traces.
8. **rliable-style reporting.** Use ≥10 seeds per condition (Agarwal et al. show that with 3 runs, in a field where 3–10 runs are prevalent, bootstrap CIs under-cover), IQM with stratified bootstrap 95% CIs, performance profiles across function families, and probability of improvement. Pre-register the cost model and refutation thresholds.
9. **Matched-compute composition evaluation.** For H3, log per-composition reuse counts on held-out tasks, and compare against the inlined reference at equal expansion budget, following the Berlot-Attwell methodology.

## Caveats

- Several citations (marked "not verified this pass") are standard and probably correct, but I did not check them against primary sources here. Check them before publication. Statements about Sigma, Clarion, LIDA, OpenCog, Icarus, NARS, the Gödel machine, OOPS and AIXI come from general knowledge.
- The exact form of Dasgupta's log-factor guarantee is stated from the paper's framing and from later restatements (Golovin & Krause). Read the paper's theorem before quoting a constant.
- The Piantadosi et al. data archive was located but not opened. Its trial-level granularity is unconfirmed.
- Specific numbers in the "derived" arguments (truth-table storage sizes, the 128× hypervector overhead, 2^(n−1) DNF terms for parity) are straightforward arithmetic or textbook facts, not results from a cited paper.
- Clarkson et al.'s JAIR 2026 publication is listed by IBM Research (dated July 2026). [ibm](https://research.ibm.com/publications/capacity-analysis-of-vector-symbolic-architectures) Capacity figures circulating in unreviewed GitHub issues should be ignored.
